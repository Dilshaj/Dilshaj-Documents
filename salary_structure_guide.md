# Salary Breakdown, Calculation & Statutory Compliance Guide

This comprehensive guide explains how each salary component is structured, calculated, and legally grounded under Indian Labour Laws. Every percentage, threshold, wage ceiling, and formula is supported with exact Acts, Sections, Central Gazette Notifications, and Supreme Court rulings.

## 2. In-Depth Component Breakdown, Legal Provisions & Proofs

### A. Cost to Company (CTC)
* **What it means**: The total annual expenditure incurred by the establishment on employing an individual.
* **Formula**: $\text{CTC} = \text{Gross Salary (Earnings)} + \text{Employer Statutory Benefits (PF, ESI, Gratuity, Insurance)}$
* **Legal Grounding**: "CTC" is a commercial human-resources contract term defined under the **Indian Contract Act, 1872**. It includes both the direct compensation paid to the employee (Gross Pay) and non-cash statutory welfare contributions paid by the company to government bodies on the employee's behalf.

---

### B. Section A: Gross Salary (Earnings)
*Gross Salary represents the total contractual earnings payable to the employee before any statutory or non-statutory deductions.*

#### 1. Basic Salary
* **Standard Formula**: **50% of Gross Salary** ($\text{Gross} \times 0.50$).
* **Statutory Proofs & Legal Rationale**:
  1. **Section 2(y) of the Code on Wages, 2019 (Act No. 29 of 2019)**:
     * Under the statutory wage definition, specified exclusions (such as HRA, conveyance, travel allowance, and performance bonus) cannot collectively exceed **50%** of the total remuneration.
     * If these allowances exceed 50%, the surplus amount is deemed by law to be part of "wages" and gets added back to the calculation base for PF and Gratuity. Setting the Basic Salary to at least 50% ensures strict compliance with this statutory threshold.
  2. **Supreme Court Landmark Ruling on Allowance Splitting**:
     * *Regional Provident Fund Commissioner (II) West Bengal vs. Vivekananda Vidyamandir and Others* **(2019) 20 SCC 780 / 2019 SCC OnLine SC 291** (decided on 28 February 2019):
     * The Hon'ble Supreme Court affirmed that any allowance that is paid universally, routinely, and ordinarily across all employees (and not specifically linked to extra productivity, overtime, or unusual effort) forms part of "Basic Wages" under **Section 2(b) of the EPF & MP Act, 1952**. Maintaining a genuine 50% Basic Salary structure protects the employer and employee against penalties for artificial wage-splitting.
  3. **Section 3 & 4 of the Minimum Wages Act, 1948**:
     * The Basic Salary must never fall below the notified statutory minimum wage rate applicable in the respective State for the relevant class of employment and skill category (Unskilled, Semi-Skilled, Skilled, or Highly Skilled).

#### 2. House Rent Allowance (HRA)
* **Standard Formula**: **40% of Basic Salary** (for Non-Metro cities) or **50% of Basic Salary** (for Metro cities: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai).
* **Statutory Proofs & Legal Rationale**:
  * **Section 10(13A) of the Income Tax Act, 1961** read with **Rule 2A of the Income Tax Rules, 1962**:
  * HRA is an allowance granted to meet expenditure actually incurred on residential accommodation. Under Rule 2A, the maximum income tax exemption is computed as the least of:
    1. Actual HRA received from the employer.
    2. **50% of Basic Salary** (in Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai) or **40% of Basic Salary** (in all other cities).
    3. Actual rent paid minus 10% of Basic Salary.
  * Aligning the HRA component directly to 40% (or 50%) of Basic optimizes tax exemption for the employee without breaching IT department caps.

#### 3. Special Allowance
* **Formula**: $\text{CTC} - \text{Basic} - \text{HRA} - \text{Employer Statutory Benefits}$
* **Statutory Nature**:
  * A flexible residual balancing allowance taxable under **Section 17(1) of the Income Tax Act, 1961**. It accommodates grade increments and ensures that the total components reconcile exactly to the offered annual CTC.

### C. Section B & C: Employees' Provident Fund (EPF)

#### 1. Why 12% Contribution?
* **Statutory Law**: **Section 6 of the Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952**:
  > *"The contribution which shall be paid by the employer to the Fund shall be **twelve per cent** of the basic wages, dearness allowance and retaining allowance (if any) for the time being payable to each of the employees... and the employee's contribution shall be equal to the contribution payable by the employer..."*
* **Employee Share**: **12% of Basic Salary** is deducted from Gross Salary and credited directly to the employee's **EPF Account (Account No. 1)**.
* **Employer Share (Total 12%) Split**:
  Under **Section 6A of the EPF & MP Act, 1952** and **Paragraph 3 of the Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS), 1995**:
  * **8.33%** is diverted into the **Employees' Pension Fund (EPS - Account No. 10)**.
    * *Statutory Cap*: Capped at 8.33% of the statutory wage limit of ₹15,000 = **₹1,250 per month** (Paragraph 3(1) of EPS, 1995).
  * **3.67%** (or remainder of 12%) is credited to the **Employees' Provident Fund (EPF - Account No. 1)**.
    * If basic salary exceeds ₹15,000 and 12% is deposited on full basic, the balance beyond ₹1,250 also goes into the EPF Account.

#### 2. What is the ₹15,000 Limit and When is PF Mandatory?
* **Statutory Wage Ceiling Proof**:
  * **Ministry of Labour and Employment Gazette Notification No. G.S.R. 603(E) dated 22nd August 2014 (effective 1st September 2014)**:
  * This official notification amended **Paragraph 2(f)** and **Paragraph 26** of the *Employees' Provident Funds Scheme, 1952*, raising the statutory wage ceiling from ₹6,500 to **₹15,000 per month**.
* **Mandatory Rule ($\le$ ₹15,000)**:
  * If an employee's Basic Salary is **₹15,000 per month or less** at the time of joining a covered establishment (having 20 or more employees), EPF membership is **statutorily mandatory by law**. Neither the employer nor the employee can opt out.
* **Above ₹15,000 (The "Excluded Employee" Rule)**:
  * An employee who joins with a Basic Salary exceeding ₹15,000 per month and who is not already a member of the EPFO is termed an **"Excluded Employee"** under **Paragraph 2(f) of the EPF Scheme, 1952**.
  * For such employees, PF enrollment is **voluntary**.
* **Voluntary PF above ₹15,000**:
  * Under **Paragraph 26(6) of the EPF Scheme, 1952**, with mutual consent between employer and employee:
    1. **Statutory Capping Approach**: PF contribution can be restricted to the statutory ceiling of ₹15,000 (i.e., fixed at 12% of ₹15,000 = **₹1,800/month** by employee and **₹1,800/month** by employer).
    2. **Full Basic Approach**: PF can be contributed at 12% of the actual uncapped Basic Salary.

#### 3. Additional Employer Levies under EPF
* **EDLI (Employees' Deposit-Linked Insurance Scheme, 1976)**:
  * **0.50%** of Basic Salary (capped at 0.50% of ₹15,000 = **maximum ₹75 per month**) under **Paragraph 28** of the EDLI Scheme, 1976 (MoLE Notification **G.S.R. 1065(E)** dated 15 February 2018).
  * Provides life insurance cover (up to ₹7,00,000) to the nominee in case of death during service.
* **EPF Administrative Charges**:
  * **0.50%** of Basic Salary (minimum ₹500 per month per establishment) under **Paragraph 38** of the EPF Scheme, 1952 (MoLE Notification **G.S.R. 496(E)** dated 21 May 2018, effective 1 June 2018).

### D. Section B & C: Employees' State Insurance (ESI)

#### 1. Why 3.25% Employer and 0.75% Employee?
* **Statutory Law**: **Section 39 & 40 of the Employees' State Insurance Act, 1948 (ESI Act, 1948)** read with **Rule 51 of the Employees' State Insurance (Central) Rules, 1950**.
* **Gazette Notification Proof**:
  * **Ministry of Labour and Employment Gazette Notification No. G.S.R. 423(E) dated 13th June 2019 (effective 1st July 2019)**:
  * Amending Rule 51 of the ESI (Central) Rules, 1950, the Central Government reduced the historic total contribution from 6.5% to **4.00%**:
    * **Employer Share**: Reduced from 4.75% to **3.25% of Gross Wages**.
    * **Employee Share**: Reduced from 1.75% to **0.75% of Gross Wages**.
    * **Total Contribution**: **4.00% of Gross Wages**.

#### 2. What is the ₹21,000 Wage Limit and When is ESI Mandatory?
* **Statutory Wage Ceiling Proof**:
  * **Ministry of Labour and Employment Gazette Notification No. S.O. 4212(E) dated 22nd December 2016 (effective 1st January 2017)**:
  * This notification amended **Rule 50 of the Employees' State Insurance (Central) Rules, 1950**, raising the wage limit for coverage under Section 2(9) of the ESI Act from ₹15,000 to **₹21,000 per month**.
  * *(For employees with disabilities, the wage ceiling is ₹25,000 per month under Notification S.O. 556(E) dated 4 March 2008).*
* **Mandatory Rule ($\le$ ₹21,000 Gross Wages)**:
  * Any employee working in a covered establishment (10 or more persons) whose **Monthly Gross Salary is ₹21,000 or less** must be enrolled under ESIC.
  * Both the employer (3.25%) and employee (0.75%) contributions are compulsory by law.
* **Above ₹21,000 Gross Wages**:
  * If an employee's Gross Salary exceeds **₹21,000 per month** at the time of recruitment, ESI is **completely non-applicable**. Zero ESI deduction is made.
* **The "Contribution Period" Extension Rule**:
  * Under the **First Proviso to Section 2(9)** and **Rule 50**: If an enrolled employee's gross monthly wage exceeds ₹21,000 after the start of a contribution period (April 1 to September 30, or October 1 to March 31), they continue to remain covered and deductions continue until the end of that specific contribution period.
* **Exemption for Low Wage Earners**:
  * Under **Rule 52** and MoLE Notification **G.S.R. 638(E)** dated 6th September 2019, employees whose average daily wage is up to **₹176 per day** (approx. ₹4,576/month) are exempted from paying their 0.75% share. The employer must still pay the 3.25% employer share.

## 4. Key Statutory Differences: PF vs. ESI Comparison

| Feature | Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) | Employees' State Insurance (ESI) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Governing Act** | Employees' Provident Funds & MP Act, 1952 | Employees' State Insurance Act, 1948 |
| **Calculation Base** | **Basic Salary + DA** (Excludes HRA & OT) | **Total Gross Salary** (All direct cash earnings) |
| **Statutory Wage Ceiling** | **₹15,000 per month** (Basic + DA) | **₹21,000 per month** (Gross Salary) |
| **Mandatory Condition** | Mandatory if Basic $\le$ ₹15,000/month | Mandatory if Gross $\le$ ₹21,000/month |
| **Above Ceiling Limit** | Voluntary under Para 26(6); can cap at ₹1,800/mo | **Completely Exempt**; 0% deduction |
| **Employee Contribution**| **12.00%** of Basic Salary | **0.75%** of Gross Salary |
| **Employer Contribution**| **12.00%** of Basic (3.67% EPF + 8.33% EPS) | **3.25%** of Gross Salary |
| **Primary Purpose** | Retirement Corpus, Pension & Life Insurance | Medical Care, Sickness, Maternity & Injury Benefits |
| **Authority Proof** | MoLE Notification G.S.R. 603(E) (01-Sep-2014) | MoLE Notification G.S.R. 423(E) (01-Jul-2019) |

## 5. Frequently Asked Legal Questions

#### Q1. Can an employee voluntarily opt out of PF?
* **Answer**: If an employee's Basic Salary is **up to ₹15,000/month**, opt-out is **legally impossible** under Section 6 and Paragraph 26 of the EPF Scheme. If the Basic Salary is greater than ₹15,000 at the time of joining and the candidate does not have an active existing EPFO UAN, they qualify as an "Excluded Employee" and may submit **Form 11** stating they do not wish to enroll.

#### Q2. What happens if Basic Salary is ₹30,000? Is PF ₹3,600 or ₹1,800?
* **Answer**: Under Paragraph 26(6) of the EPF Scheme, the employer and employee have two legally valid options:
  1. **Statutory Wage Ceiling Option**: Restrict deduction to 12% of the statutory ceiling of ₹15,000 = **₹1,800 per month** each.
  2. **Full Wage Option**: Contribute 12% on the full basic wages (12% of ₹30,000 = **₹3,600 per month** each). Note that even on full basic, the EPS pension contribution remains capped at ₹1,250 (8.33% of ₹15,000), with the entire remaining employer share (₹2,350) routed into EPF Account No. 1.

#### Q3. Can ESI be deducted if Monthly Gross Salary is ₹25,000?
* **Answer**: **No.** Under Rule 50 of the ESI Central Rules (Notification S.O. 4212(E)), the statutory ceiling is strictly ₹21,000 per month. Any employee earning more than ₹21,000 Gross at the time of recruitment cannot be enrolled in ESI.

#### Q4. Why is Basic Salary structured at 50% rather than 30% or 40%?
* **Answer**: In the landmark judgment *RPFC v. Vivekananda Vidyamandir (2019)*, the Supreme Court clamped down on artificial wage structuring where basic salary was reduced to artificially low percentages to evade PF. Furthermore, **Section 2(y) of the Code on Wages, 2019** limits all exclusions (allowances) to a maximum of 50% of total remuneration. Maintaining Basic at 50% ensures long-term statutory compliance across both existing case law and the new labour codes.

#### Q5. Is PF applicable during Probation? Is it legal to skip PF during probation, and why shouldn't an employer skip it?
* **Answer**: **NO, it is strictly ILLEGAL to skip PF during probation.**
  * **Statutory Definition**: Under **Section 2(f) of the EPF & MP Act, 1952**, an *"employee"* is defined as any person employed for wages in connection with the establishment. The statute makes **no distinction whatsoever** between a permanent employee, temporary employee, or an employee on probation.
  * **Day 1 Membership**: Under **Paragraph 26(1) of the Employees' Provident Funds Scheme, 1952**, every employee must become a member of the Fund from the **very first day of joining**. *(Historical note: The earlier qualifying period of 60/120 days was repealed decades ago by Central Government notification in 1990).*
  * **Consequences of Skipping**:
    1. **Penal Damages & Interest**: If an employer skips PF during probation, EPFO conducts an inquiry under **Section 7A** and orders backdated recovery along with **12% per annum simple interest** under **Section 7Q** plus penal damages up to **25%** under **Section 14B**.
    2. **Criminal Prosecution**: Under **Section 14 & 14(1A)** of the EPF & MP Act, 1952, non-payment of statutory contributions is a punishable criminal offence punishable with imprisonment up to 3 years.
    3. Even if an employee leaves during or after probation, the accumulated PF corpus belongs to them and can be transferred seamlessly to their next employer via their Universal Account Number (UAN).

#### Q6. Should the PF amount deposited in the EPFO account match the monthly salary slip exactly? Can they vary?
* **Answer**: **YES, the PF figure deposited with the EPFO must match the monthly salary slip EXACTLY (subject only to a ±₹1 rounding rule).**
  * **Statutory Duty**: Under **Paragraph 32 and Paragraph 38 of the EPF Scheme, 1952**, the employer is authorized to deduct the employee's contribution from their wages and must remit that exact deduction along with the employer's matching contribution to the EPFO within 15 days of the close of the month.
  * **Criminal Breach of Trust**: Under **Explanation 1 & 2 to Section 405 of the Indian Penal Code (IPC)** *(now Section 316 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, BNS)*, any employer who deducts the employee's contribution from their payslip and fails to deposit that exact amount into the employee's EPFO account is guilty of **Criminal Breach of Trust** (a non-bailable criminal offense).
  * **Allowed Variance (Rounding Off Only)**: Under **Paragraph 38(1) of the EPF Scheme, 1952**, contributions are rounded to the nearest whole rupee (amounts of 50 paise and above are rounded up to the nearest rupee, and amounts below 50 paise are ignored). Therefore, a variance of more than **₹1** is legally impermissible.
  * **Electronic Reconciliation (ECR)**: Every month, the employer uploads an Electronic Challan cum Return (ECR) to the EPFO portal specifying each employee's UAN, Gross Wages, EPF Wages, and exact 12% contribution. If the payslip deduction differs from the ECR challan, it creates an immediate audit discrepancy and tax mismatch (Form 16 vs. EPFO Member Passbook).
